FACULTY REVIEW / EDUCATIONAL ACTIVITY

Teach the next step.

Ask whether students can distinguish a recording from a claim, explain their certainty, and use evidence to choose an action.

Prepared for Dr. Alan Franklin and Dr. Alex Varakin at EKU, with Prof. Belle Woodward at SIU. This is separate from the SIB research protocol and does not collect research responses.

Review status: ready for a faculty walkthrough. Before broad student use, review the media provenance and reuse permissions and complete physical-phone and screen-reader checks. Original-audio clips still need verified captions/transcripts. The student route uses sound-free excerpts and text alternatives, with unscored authenticity predictions.

How to facilitate

  1. Let students answer the starting scenario privately. Avoid grading their first impressions.
  2. For the two visual observations, ask what the excerpt establishes and what is missing. The text route supports the same questions. Do not announce unverified authentic/fake labels.
  3. Let students open evidence cards in any order. Opening a card records exposure, not proof that they read or understood it.
  4. For the fictional library case, distinguish camera-recording provenance (record A), date/context (record B), and current class status (record C).
  5. For the caption task, separate manipulation pressure from evidence of falsity. Popularity and urgency warrant checking, not automatic rejection.
  6. Use the unfamiliar shuttle case to discuss shared-source reposts and unresolved recording provenance.
  7. At the end, ask students to connect their earlier reason with a specific next habit. Written explanations are saved for discussion, not machine-graded.

A confident error is a chance to examine reasoning. It should not lead to ridicule, blanket distrust, or the claim that visual cues reliably diagnose AI.

Before restoring audio or authenticity scoring

  • Record the original publisher, title/date, canonical URL, and full recording.
  • For an altered clip, document the creation/alteration record and precisely which visual or audio elements changed.
  • Resolve the inherited label against those records; replace examples if the evidence is inadequate.
  • Review captions against the original audio, including meaningful sounds, and provide descriptive transcripts.
  • Confirm permission or an appropriate basis for reuse with the institution.

The compressed clips cannot establish fine-grained artifact validity. This activity does not teach a visual “deepfake spotting” checklist.

Learning checks and a practical pilot

The single starting question and three final questions differ in content and length. Do not subtract their scores or call that change a learning gain. Final-check retries may reflect answer memory. First-attempt performance and every written justification appear separately in the printable reflection.

  1. Conduct a small usability pilot with 6–8 undergraduates. Observe hesitation, help requests, missing evidence, and repetitive steps. Ask which moments were interesting, confusing, or uncomfortable.
  2. Test keyboard navigation and screen-reader feedback. Test real iOS and Android devices, portrait/landscape, enlarged text, and the text-only route. Do not treat CSS responsiveness as proof of mobile usability.
  3. Measure actual completion time; “15–20 minutes” is provisional.
  4. Use equivalent unfamiliar pre/post tasks for a separate evaluation, with a planned rubric for confidence/correctness, authenticity/claim truth, and useful verification. Include genuine and manipulated material if assessing authenticity, with verified labels.
  5. Check unintended effects: blanket distrust, unsupported detection confidence, and embarrassment. Assess delayed transfer if making retention claims.
  6. Use a comparison condition and suitable research governance before making causal effectiveness claims. The current activity has no submission or analytics service.

TEXT-ONLY LEARNING ROUTE / NO JAVASCRIPT NEEDED

Practice with evidence

This route covers the same learning goals without requiring video, hearing, or interactive controls. Discuss or write responses privately.

1 / Confidence is a prediction

A student feels 95% certain a campus service has permanently closed. A current operator notice says the service is running. What does this tell you about confidence and correctness? What would you investigate next?

Discuss the reasoning

Confidence describes certainty; evidence determines whether the closure claim is supported. A confident error is a reason to revisit that judgment, not to distrust all future information.

2 / The fictional library case

A post says a short video of water outside a building proves classes are cancelled today.

  • Record A: the original camera recording was published by the fictional Lakeside Library team in May 2024. The case stipulates no AI alteration.
  • Record B: the full recording shows a planned drainage test, not building flooding; the repost omits that context and date.
  • Record C: the fictional campus’s current notice says classes run normally.

What does each record establish? Does authentic footage make the new caption true? What should you share or hold back?

Discuss the reasoning

The case supports a camera recording but not the new cancellation claim. Keep provenance, old context, and current status separate. Hold off on the false cancellation claim; a correction should link the original context and current notice.

3 / Pressure is not proof

Compare “Thousands shared this—repost before it disappears!” with “Can anyone provide the original recording and date?” Which encourages investigation? Does emotional wording alone establish falsity? What should publishers and platforms make easier?

Discuss the reasoning

Urgency and popularity pressure action; requesting a source supports checking. Neither wording establishes truth. Platforms and publishers can make original dates, sources, and corrections easier to find.

4 / The unfamiliar shuttle case

  • Two accounts repeat one anonymous uploader’s clip and claim permanent shuttle closure.
  • The current operator notice says one stop is temporarily closed and service continues on a detour.
  • The original clip’s provenance is unknown.

Are the reposts independent corroboration? What can you conclude about the closure claim? What cannot you conclude about authenticity? Name your next action and one unresolved question.

Discuss the reasoning

Two copies of one uploader are not independent confirmation. The operator notice contradicts permanent closure. Video authenticity remains unresolved. Use the current notice to correct the service claim and seek the original before claiming AI alteration.

5 / Take it with you

Write one earlier assumption, the evidence that changed or qualified it, and a specific habit you will use before sharing. These responses can demonstrate understanding; they do not establish that the activity caused learning.

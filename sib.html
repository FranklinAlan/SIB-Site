SIB / A LEARNING CHALLENGE · ABOUT 15 MINUTES

Can you trust
your confidence?

Five clips. A few surprising decisions. Discover how your confidence compares with your judgments—and practice deciding what to do next.

Your mission

By the end, you should be able to:

  • Explain why feeling certain does not guarantee a correct judgment.
  • Separate a clip’s authenticity from the truth of its message.
  • Choose a useful verification step before sharing a surprising clip.

Practice, feedback, and reflection help you work toward these goals. The final check asks you to apply them.

FINAL MISSION / BEFORE YOU SHARE

A convincing clip lands in your feed.

A fictional post says: “This proves it! Everyone is sharing it. Repost before it gets deleted.” You have not found the original source.

What is your best next move?

Appearance, popularity, and emotional wording do not establish authenticity or truth. Trace the original recording, check its date and full context, and look for independent corroboration. If evidence is insufficient, holding off on sharing is a useful decision.

FINAL CHECK / USE WHAT YOU LEARNED

Three ideas to take with you.

Choose an answer for each question. Feedback will explain the reasoning.

1. You were 95% confident and your judgment was wrong. What follows?
2. An authentic video has a misleading caption. Is its message necessarily true?
3. A surprising clip has no identifiable original source. What should you do?

LEARN THROUGH DOING

What this activity is for

SIB helps students practice thoughtful judgments about digital media. The goal is to recognize uncertainty, reflect on confidence, and choose a useful next action.

This activity is prepared for review by Dr. Alan Franklin and Dr. Alex Varakin at EKU, with Prof. Belle Woodward at SIU. Student learning is the priority: the activities, feedback, and final check are designed around the three learning goals above.

The final check provides feedback on these ideas. It does not establish that the activity caused learning or measure your overall ability to detect deepfakes.

How SIB connects to research

Seeing Isn’t Believing explores confidence, reflection, and agency under uncertainty in AI-mediated media. This educational activity is separate from the SIB study protocol and does not collect research responses.

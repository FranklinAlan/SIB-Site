Case A · The campus reading-room poll

Which seating did respondents prefer?

A fictional campus poll asked 120 students to select one preferred seating option for a reading room. All 120 answered: 72 selected movable chairs and 48 selected fixed desks. The poll used volunteers from a campus mailing list and did not sample all enrolled students.

Claim: “Most respondents in this poll preferred movable chairs to fixed desks.”

Your judgment for Case A

Case B · A note-taking comparison

What do the recorded averages show?

In a fictional class, 48 volunteers chose to type or handwrite notes on the same lesson. Both groups later took the same 20-word recall test. Typed-note participants recalled an average of 16 words; handwritten-note participants recalled an average of 12. The report did not adjust for differences between the groups.

Claim: “The handwritten-note group recalled more words on average than the typed-note group in this comparison.”

Your judgment for Case B

Case C · Music and practice questions

What explains the performance difference?

In a fictional study of 60 volunteers, students chose to practice in silence or with music. They completed the same quiz. The silence group averaged 84%; the music group averaged 76%. Students in silence also reported sleeping longer the night before. The analysis did not adjust for sleep or other differences.

Claim: “Practicing in silence caused the higher quiz average in this study.”

Your judgment for Case C

Case D · Sources and a responsible update

Should the library stay open later?

Use only this new packet. Show how the reports connect to their evidence, then write what you could responsibly tell other students.

Campus News reports that 122 of 200 volunteers favored later library hours, citing poll Q17. The Student Union newsletter reports the same finding and also cites Q17.

Campus Radio reports that 81 of 150 volunteers favored later hours, citing R09. R09 was conducted separately by another team with different respondents. Neither poll used a representative sample of the university’s students.

Claim: “Most students across the university favor later library hours.”

Evidence and design inferences

The sources below were checked in the preceding review. They support design rationales in their own contexts. This update is not a new systematic review and does not establish the effectiveness of this game.

  • Chi, de Leeuw, Chiu, and LaVancher (1994), Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding. Relevant article sections checked. Prompted explanations supported learning of a science text; the short judgment explanation here is a different activity.
  • Butler and Roediger (2008), Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing. Relevant article sections checked. Feedback reduced later lure intrusions. The decision to emphasize corrective headings is a design inference, not a tested result about this interface.
  • Wineburg and McGrew (2019), Lateral reading and the nature of expertise. Publisher abstract checked. Expert checking beyond the original site differs from this supplied-source simulation.
  • Butler (2010), Repeated testing produces superior transfer of learning relative to repeated studying. Abstract and relevant article sections checked. Supports separating new inferential questions and delayed application from practiced-answer recall; no effect size is imported into this game.
  • Schwartz and Bransford (1998), A time for telling. Relevant article sections checked. College students analyzed contrasting psychology cases before explanation. This supports a rationale for meaningful contrasts, not a validated effect of the proposed brief debrief.

Student authorship and reduced reflection burden are alignment and usability proposals to test. Reflection choice and response length are not learning scores.

Connection to SIB and earlier reviews

The supplied SIB Educational Pilot Rationale, Design and Methods Updated and SIB Numbered Article Syntheses and Four Point Summaries support preserving original reasoning and permitting justified retention or reconsideration. The original SIB elicited participant-generated contrasts across judgment experiences. This game does not reproduce structured PCT elicitation, measure confidence calibration, or test a PCT mechanism.

The earlier 64-source review informed versions 3.0 and 3.1. Its reported access coverage was 53 research publications plus 11 resources: 25 research sources through relevant full-text sections, 6 through substantial excerpts, and 22 through abstracts; 8 substantive resource guides and 3 overviews. Those numbers do not mean 64 fully read papers. This version does not claim to have reread all those sources.

Records and version changes

Game and assessment use separate new 3.2 storage keys. Old records are not migrated or relabelled. First explanation, later choices, first/final evidence-note drafts, reflection response mode, and assessment D fields are distinct in exports. Assessment skips are missing, not wrong; paper/oral reports are distinct from typed omissions and have no automatic facilitator verification.

Verification

Functional verification completed on 2 November 2026. These checks establish software behavior, not learning effectiveness.

  • JavaScript syntax and state-transition checks passed. Automated checks covered 5,616 complete game paths across initial judgments, supplied choices, provided/skipped writing, both editorial decisions, and all reflection choices and modes. Additional checks covered first-response preservation, revision records, invalid-action guards, reload state, text escaping, CSV escaping, and separation from older storage versions.
  • Assessment checks covered all 16 answered/skipped case combinations, required Case D fields, locked submissions, and four-row exports. Skipped responses remain missing rather than incorrect.
  • A Chromium walkthrough verified that supplied reasons appear after the original explanation is saved, original writing survives reload, misleading reasoning receives an explicit correction, an unsaved note revision blocks the final action, and the first and revised notes stay separate. Paper/oral reflection controls and the required completion self-report were checked; typed reflection drafts survived mode switching.
  • Downloaded game CSV/JSON records retained the original explanation, first and final evidence notes, revision count, and both reflection responses. Downloaded assessment records retained Case D source tracing and writing, assistance mode, and a skipped case as missing. Assessment drafts survived reload before saving.
  • Visual checks at a 390-pixel embedded viewport found the landing page and notebook readable; Escape closed the notebook and restored focus to its opener. This is a responsive-layout check, not testing on a physical phone or with assistive technology.

Still requires classroom checking: screen-reader behavior, a full keyboard-only walkthrough, actual mobile devices, browser-specific local-file storage, printer/PDF pagination, completion times, and learning outcomes. The print control was invoked, but this browser did not expose a verifiable print preview or PDF. Pilot these before routine use.

Functional checks do not demonstrate educational effectiveness. Check actual classroom phones, assistive technology, printing, timing, and response burden before a full class rollout.

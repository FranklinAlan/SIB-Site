WHAT CHANGED IN 3.1
- Write an explanation of the causal judgment before feedback, or explicitly skip it.
- Revisit that explanation after the editorial decision. Retain, clarify, revise, or remain unsure.
- Save three reflection responses, or explicitly skip the reflection. No reflection choice is scored.
- Original and later reasoning are separate in the notebook, print report, CSV, and JSON.
- Draft writing saves as you type. A new game storage key separates version 3.1 from 3.0 records.

RETAINED FROM 3.0
- Students judge a supported descriptive finding alongside an unresolved causal claim.
- Reason and headline choices are similar in length and tone.
- Students select an accurate headline and decisive evidence before seeing their completed bulletin.
- Two short posts trace to one fictional study.
- Three unfamiliar assessment cases are separate from gameplay and provide no hints or feedback.
- Original first responses remain saved; headline revisions do not overwrite them.
- Optional hints, untimed play, the lab design, and a short instructor debrief remain.

CLASSROOM USE
Read teacher-guide.html first. Students make individual first responses.
If using the independent cases, give assessment.html after the game and reflection, and BEFORE
peer discussion or the debrief. It is intentionally not an automatic game stage.
Then debrief with the contrasting example in the guide.

Plan 20–25 minutes of game and reflection time, roughly 5–8 minutes for the optional cases,
and 3–5 minutes of discussion. The proposed full research session needs additional baseline and follow-up materials. These are estimates to check during a walkthrough.
All case details are fictional. This is guided evidence evaluation, not a secure exam.

RECORDS
Game and assessment save separately in this browser. Nothing is uploaded or
submitted automatically. Use CSV, JSON, or printing when you need a retained
record under your authorized classroom/research procedure. No name is requested;
the assessment offers an optional instructor-assigned code.

A game CSV has one row. Original explanation and reflection fields are separate.
editorial_completed records the final action. reflection_status is not_started, pending,
saved, or skipped. case_completed means editorial completion plus saved/skipped reflection;
it does not measure learning. Missing writing is not an incorrect answer. An assessment CSV has three rows for ONE student's
three cases. Optional experience ratings repeat on those three rows; analyze
those ratings once per student. These are not three independent participants.

Start over resets the game. New attempt resets the assessment. Save needed
records before resetting. Game version 3.1 uses its own storage key; it does not overwrite or import version 3.0
responses. Export any needed old records from the old version before replacing files.
The unchanged assessment remains version 3.0 and retains its existing storage key.
The new game does not delete older-version browser records. On shared devices, also
clear any older records through the browser/site data controls when authorized.

EVALUATION LIMITS
This is the first workshop refinement. It does not yet include the matched plain lesson,
three expanded assessment forms, baseline/follow-up scheduling, or automatic data collection.
The game is practice. A first pilot can describe feasibility and performance
on these few fresh cases. It cannot establish improvement, broad media literacy,
calibration, lasting transfer, or the benefit of the game format on its own.
No confidence scale, PCT elicitation, extra stations, or click-history logging
has been added. See the guide for the proposed reason rubric and study limits.
